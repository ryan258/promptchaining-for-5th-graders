# Launch and pilot plan

**Campaign** · Make the next step better.

**First audience** · Fifth-grade teachers and instructional coaches who can run a short, projected activity on an approved computer.

**First ask** · Preview and lead **Rescue rainy recess**.

**Status** · Proposed owner-run pilot. All timings, sample sizes, and decision thresholds below are planning assumptions.

## Offer and paths

Lead with a complete classroom experience. A teacher projects one fictional problem. Students make a choice from the clues, fix a prepared answer together, and find their exact words in the next prompt. The prepared example at the end gives the class something to compare and discuss. Plan for 10–15 minutes of class time after setup. Confirm the time in a rehearsal.

The first outreach asks educators to try Rainy Recess. The mascot mystery gives teachers a follow-up for reasoning about uncertainty. The paper bridge gives them a follow-up for testing whether directions can be followed. Family activities extend the lesson when a teacher or caregiver wants one.

Give independent practitioners a separate entry. They can inspect the authored Moon walkthrough or use the fictional Decision memo case before they decide whether to configure OpenRouter. Introduce the live Workbench through one repeated task, a short recipe, a review pause, and a saved result.

| Stage | Classroom next step | Practitioner next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | See the exact correction carry into the next prompt in a short, captioned demo. | See a saved answer inside the next prompt in the Moon walkthrough. |
| Evaluate | Read the one-screen facilitator plan and setup requirements. | Review the local install, model setup and data path before using personal material. |
| Try | Rehearse **Rescue rainy recess** on the intended computer, then lead it. | Open the Moon example or complete one fictional professional case. |
| Return | Pick the mascot or bridge challenge based on what students need to practice. | Reopen a saved run in **My library**, then save the recipe or export the result. |

## Release sequence

Use elapsed stages so a scheduling change never creates a promise to the audience. One rehearsal, one invitation, and one feedback request per participant are enough for the first pass. The maintainer can run each stage alone with a host teacher. A coach or technology lead joins where local procedure calls for it.

| When | Person and channel | Asset or action | Ready when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before inviting | Maintainer on the intended demonstration computer | Use the classroom rehearsal in the [copy kit](COPY-KIT.md). Capture the version, installation effort, display/keyboard friction, and actual activity time. Rehearse with fictional text. | A facilitator can get from Home to a saved fix and the prepared example with the projector legible. |
| Before inviting | Maintainer, then host teacher or coach | Share the activity overview, setup instructions and data path with the adult who can approve the trial. Decide who runs the machine and which local workspace is appropriate for a shared demonstration. | Distribution terms, working handoff link or owner-led demo format, and host expectations are clear. |
| Invitation | Maintainer by direct email to an educator or coach known to them | Send the short pilot invitation from the [press and partner kit](PRESS-AND-PARTNER-KIT.md). Offer a projected rehearsal before class. | One adult accepts and knows how to try it. |
| First session | Host teacher or coach on a projected screen | Lead Rainy Recess. Use pairs or whole-class discussion. Have the adult enter one fictional class correction. Ask the exit prompt below. | The teacher can show the same words in both places and report what students could explain. |
| Within two school days | Maintainer in the existing email thread | Send the five-line reply template. Offer the mascot or bridge follow-up if the class is interested. | The tally, any blocker, and the next requested action are recorded. |
| After the first session | Maintainer reviewing with the host | Fix the largest friction point. Invite two to four more adult facilitators if setup and the first lesson hold up. | The largest issue has a clear resolution or the next rehearsal can isolate it. |
| After the pilot | Maintainer on a chosen owned channel, if sharing is appropriate | Lead with the short Rainy Recess demo and the classroom page. Publish the Workbench copy where independent practitioners already look for project documentation. | An adult reader can follow the invitation to a verified destination with a clear setup path. |

Use a known educator or coach relationship before broad distribution. A school newsletter or professional educator community is a follow-up route once the facilitator path works and the channel welcomes the material. An owned project note or documentation page fits Workbench discovery. One durable demo and a short URL in the eventual materials are easier to maintain than channel-specific campaigns.

## What to measure

Start with one readiness session and a proposed pilot of **three to five facilitators**, including the first if their rehearsal leads to a session. Treat the facilitator as the unit for setup and completion. For the exit prompt, report the number of answers actually sampled. Keep response counts and denominators together. In a small pilot, one repeated problem carries more information than a percentage.

| Question | What to count or observe | Collection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can an adult get started? | Number who open Rainy Recess on the intended computer without maintainer intervention, out of facilitators who attempted setup. Note active setup minutes and where it stopped. | Facilitator tally after rehearsal. |
| Can a class make the handoff? | Number of sessions in which the facilitator saved a class fix, the group found its words in the next prompt, and the facilitator reached the prepared example, out of sessions begun. | Yes/no/partial per session, with the stuck screen if partial. |
| Did participants notice the mechanism? | For an anonymous sample of exit responses, tally answers that identify both what moved forward and what the next prompt asked the AI to do. Tally a clue-backed correction separately. | Teacher tallies oral answers or reads anonymous slips locally. |
| Is it usable in context? | Where students or adults waited, needed instructions repeated, or could not see, hear, or operate the controls. Record whether the teacher could keep the group participating with the available methods. | One facilitator note, including participation method if useful. |
| Does someone use it again? | Number of facilitators who report a second challenge or a second class within two weeks, out of those who report a completed first session. | One opt-in follow-up. |
| Can an adult resume in Workbench? | In a separate walkthrough, can a practitioner open the authored example, locate a prompt handoff, return to the saved run from **My library**, and find the trace export? | One or two optional adult rehearsals using fictional data. |

**Exit prompt.** “What words did we change? Where did they show up next? What did that next prompt ask for?” For the clue check ask “Which clue backs up our fix?” Accept pointing, oral explanation, drawing or a short written answer.

**One-line private pilot log.** `Date | anonymous session ID | setup independent/assisted/blocked + minutes | reached comparison Y/N | handoff correct __ of __ | clue supported __ of __ | friction | retry/next action`

**Five-line adult reply template.** The facilitator can reply by voice or with brief phrases.

1. Opened Rainy Recess independently, with help, or blocked. Setup minutes if known.
2. Saved a class fix and reached the comparison, yes or no.
3. Class sample that explained the handoff, __ of __. Sample that named a supporting clue, __ of __.
4. One moment that slowed you down or made participation hard.
5. Would you use another challenge? If yes, which one?

Use anonymous session IDs and aggregate counts. Keep student names, recordings, identifiable quotations, and screenshots of class work outside the pilot log and promotional material. Capture a screenshot for marketing using a fictional demonstration run. Keep the adult contact list apart from the tally and retain the short aggregate after the pilot according to the host's expectations. A teacher's sample is descriptive and can miss quieter students. Report the sample and context with any result Ryan later elects to share.

## Advance, revise, or pause

- **Advance to a small second round** when the first facilitator can reach the comparison on their intended setup, the class sees the handoff, and the host can name a practical next use.
- **Revise and rehearse** when two facilitators hit the same setup, reading, or action problem. Fix the first blocking step and ask one facilitator to repeat it.
- **Pause classroom outreach** when the intended school equipment cannot run the local app, the host cannot use the planned setup, or participants cannot access the activity with the available alternatives. Offer a facilitator-led demo if the host chooses that format.
- **Consider wider sharing** when at least three facilitators have reached the comparison, the handoff is explainable in the sampled responses, and an unfamiliar adult has successfully followed the setup instructions on an intended device. This is a proposed pilot decision threshold. Keep classroom learning, curriculum fit, accessibility conformance, and independent student use as separate claims that require their own evidence.
